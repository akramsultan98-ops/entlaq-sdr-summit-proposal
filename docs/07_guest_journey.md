# Guest Journey — ENTLAQ SDR Summit 2026

**Purpose:** Define the guest's end-to-end experience of the Summit — the arc from invitation to departure, and how it is shaped differently for each tier.

> **Scope boundary.** This document owns the **experience arc and the connective narrative between touchpoints**. It does not restate the mechanics that sit elsewhere: vehicle and fleet operations ([06_transportation_strategy.md](06_transportation_strategy.md)), accreditation and badging mechanics ([08_registration_strategy.md](08_registration_strategy.md)), VVIP protocol and concierge service design ([09_vip_experience.md](09_vip_experience.md)), venue selection and spatial requirements ([05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md)), or accommodation standards ([03_accommodation.md](03_accommodation.md)). Where those documents own a touchpoint, this one places it in sequence and defines what the guest should *feel* there.

---

# 1. Objective

The RFP asks for "a seamless, premium guest experience for VVIP, VIP, and standard tiers across every touchpoint." That is a journey requirement, not a production requirement — it is delivered in the spaces *between* the programme, not during it.

**What the guest journey must achieve:**

- **Carry the three-act narrative.** The RFP's design logic is *Diagnose → Dive → Do*. The guest should feel the shift between acts, not just read it in an agenda. Day 1 should land with weight, Day 2 should feel like work, Day 3 should feel like commitment.
- **Make ~500 people feel individually expected.** The Summit is invitation-led. Every guest was chosen. The journey should reflect that at every touchpoint, particularly the first.
- **Deliver tiering without creating visible hierarchy.** VVIP, VIP and standard guests share the same Summit. Differentiation must be felt by those receiving it and invisible to those who are not — a standard guest should never experience their tier as a deficiency.
- **Manage energy across three days.** The programme runs late evenings, early mornings and a dawn start on Day 3, in September heat. Guest energy is a finite resource and the journey either conserves it or exhausts it.
- **Convert attendance into outcomes.** The RFP's purpose is policy momentum, capital connections and concrete commitments. Those happen in the informal spaces — breaks, brunches, lounges, transfers. The journey design is what makes those encounters likely rather than accidental.

**Governing principle:** the journey should be designed so that **the guest never has to ask what happens next**. Every point of uncertainty is a point of friction, and friction at senior level reads as disorganisation.

# 3. Journey Principles

Six principles govern every touchpoint decision.

| # | Principle | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Anticipate, don't react** | The guest should find things already prepared — room ready, badge waiting, name known. Service that responds quickly is good; service that was already there is premium. |
| 2 | **One source of truth** | The Entlaq app is established by the RFP as the digital layer. It should be the single place a guest looks for what happens next, reinforced by physical wayfinding — never contradicted by it. |
| 3 | **Differentiate quietly** | Tiering is delivered through access, timing and attention — not through visible symbols of exclusion. Separate routes, not roped-off queues. |
| 4 | **Protect the informal** | The RFP's value is created in matchmaking, breaks and brunches. These are not gaps between sessions; they are the commercial core and should be designed with the same care as the main stage. |
| 5 | **Design for the tired guest** | By Day 3 a guest has had two late nights and a dawn start. Late-journey touchpoints need more support, not less. |
| 6 | **Close as deliberately as you open** | Departure is the last impression and the one most often neglected. It carries disproportionate weight in how the Summit is remembered. |

# 4. The Journey Arc

Seven phases, from before arrival to after departure.

## Phase 1 — Invitation & Anticipation *(pre-event)*

**What happens:** The guest receives the invitation, accepts, and receives pre-arrival information — travel details, programme, app onboarding, practical guidance.

**What the guest should feel:** Selected. The Summit is invitation-led and the invitation itself is the first brand moment.

**Journey requirements:** A single, well-designed pre-arrival communication stream rather than fragmented emails. Clarity on what is covered and what is not — particularly accommodation, given that per [03_accommodation.md](03_accommodation.md) roughly 70% of guests are not in the Entlaq block and must arrange their own stay. Early app onboarding so matchmaking can begin before arrival, maximising the value of the time on site.

**Risk if neglected:** Guests arrive uncertain about basic logistics, and the on-site team spends Day 1 answering questions that should have been closed a week earlier.

---

## Phase 2 — Arrival *(Day 1, daytime — staggered)*

**What happens:** Airport arrival, transfer, hotel check-in, accreditation and welcome reception.

**What the guest should feel:** Expected. Received, not processed.

**Journey requirements:** The RFP describes staggered arrivals, so the arrival experience must be **sustained rather than staged** — the guest arriving at 16:00 should receive what the 10:00 guest received. Arrival is where three separately-owned processes meet: transfer, check-in and accreditation. From the guest's perspective these are one continuous moment, and the journey design's job is to make the seams invisible.

**Critical dependency:** Heat. Arrival happens in the daytime in September. Every arrival touchpoint — vehicle, reception, welcome area — must be shaded and cooled, or the first physical impression of the Summit is discomfort.

**Owned elsewhere:** vehicle operations ([06](06_transportation_strategy.md)); accreditation mechanics ([08](08_registration_strategy.md)); VVIP meet-and-greet ([09](09_vip_experience.md)).

---

## Phase 3 — The Opening *(Day 1, evening)*

**What happens:** The evening main-stage programme and the report launch — the Summit's signature moment.

**What the guest should feel:** That they are present at something consequential. This is the emotional peak of the entire Summit.

**Journey requirements:** The approach to the venue matters as much as the reveal itself — guests should arrive into a sense of occasion, not into a queue. Seating should be resolved before guests arrive rather than negotiated at the door. The live polling described in the RFP is the mechanism that converts an audience into participants; onboarding for it must be frictionless, because a guest who cannot make polling work becomes a spectator at the moment they were meant to be engaged.

**The journey's hardest transition follows immediately:** from report launch to gala. See §5.

---

## Phase 4 — The Working Day *(Day 2)*

**What happens:** Morning roundtables, midday brunch, evening main stage, marina dinner.

**What the guest should feel:** Productive. Day 2 is where the Summit earns its commercial reputation.

**Journey requirements:** This is the day with the most transitions and the highest risk of guest fragmentation — different guests attend different sector roundtables and different matchmaking sessions, so the shared experience thins. The journey must provide **regathering moments**: brunch and the evening main stage are where the group becomes one audience again, and both should be designed to feel like reunions rather than logistics.

Matchmaking sits at the centre of Day 2's value. Per the RFP it is app-driven, which means the journey depends on the app working well under load and on guests being able to find their counterpart easily — a wayfinding and space-design requirement as much as a digital one.

**Energy note:** Day 2 ends late at the marina after starting early at the roundtables. Attendance at the marina dinner is the most fragile in the programme.

---

## Phase 5 — The Close *(Day 3, morning to midday)*

**What happens:** Sunrise wellness, workshops, midday main-stage close with potential announcements.

**What the guest should feel:** Committed. The RFP's third act is "Do" — the guest should leave with something concrete.

**Journey requirements:** Day 3 asks the most of guests and gives them the least recovery time — a dawn start after two late nights, then a fragmented morning, then a midday session. **This is the phase where journey design matters most and is most likely to be under-resourced.** Wellness participation will be self-selecting and modest; workshops require guests to have chosen and arrived; the close requires them to still be present.

The close is also where guests begin mentally departing. Practical departure information must be resolved *before* the close begins, so the final session is experienced rather than endured by guests worrying about flights.

**Note:** the conditions of the midday close at SIA are the subject of a Site Inspection Priority assessment in [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md), which will determine what guest comfort provision is required.

---

## Phase 6 — Departure *(Day 3, mid-afternoon)*

**What happens:** Farewell and departures by mid-afternoon per the RFP.

**What the guest should feel:** Concluded, not evicted.

**Journey requirements:** Departure is compressed — ~500 guests leaving in a narrow window against fixed flight times. The journey requirement is that the guest experiences a **farewell**, not a scramble: a defined closing moment, clear departure information delivered in advance, and somewhere comfortable to wait for guests whose flights are later than the programme's end. A guest turned out of a room with hours to wait ends the Summit on its weakest note.

**Owned elsewhere:** departure wave logistics ([06](06_transportation_strategy.md)); post-checkout holding provision ([03](03_accommodation.md)).

---

## Phase 7 — Follow-Through *(post-event)*

**What happens:** Content access, matchmaking follow-up, commitments converted.

**What the guest should feel:** That the Summit continues to be useful.

**Journey requirements:** The RFP establishes that the app provides content access, which means the guest relationship does not end at departure. The Summit's stated purpose — commitments and capital connections — is realised weeks after the event, not during it. The journey should close with a clear handover into whatever follow-up Entlaq intends.

**Open point:** the RFP does not define post-event follow-up. Whether it falls to the agency is undefined.

# 5. Moments That Matter

Six touchpoints carry disproportionate weight. Each is a point where the Summit is either made or visibly compromised.

| # | Moment | Why it carries weight | Design imperative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **First contact at the airport** | The first physical proof of whether this is a well-run event, delivered when the guest is most tired | Sustained presence; the guest is met, not collected |
| 2 | **Accreditation** | The moment the guest becomes a participant; also where tiering first becomes visible | Fast, pre-prepared, tier-appropriate; never a queue for senior guests |
| 3 | **The report launch reveal** | The Summit's stated signature moment and the reason the audience travelled | Every seat must see it; participation must work first time |
| 4 | **Report launch → gala transition** | The highest-risk moment in the journey: the emotional peak is immediately followed by a venue change | Design the transition as part of the experience, not as a gap between two experiences |
| 5 | **First matchmaking meeting** | Where the Summit's commercial promise either materialises or does not | Easy to find, easy to start, comfortable enough to continue |
| 6 | **The farewell** | The last impression, disproportionately weighted in memory | A deliberate close, not a dissolve into departure logistics |

**The single highest-risk moment is #4.** Per [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md), the gala is at a separate venue from the main stage. ~300 guests move directly from the Summit's emotional peak into a transfer. Every minute of that transition either sustains or dissipates what the report launch created. It should be treated as a designed sequence — continuity of music, lighting, hosting and pace — rather than as a transport movement with an event at each end.

# 6. Journey by Tier

How the same journey differs across the three tiers the RFP defines. This is the experiential shape only — VVIP and VIP service design is owned by [09_vip_experience.md](09_vip_experience.md).

| Phase | Standard | VIP | VVIP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-arrival** | Programme, practical info, app onboarding | As standard, plus personalised programme guidance | Individually managed; protocol contact established |
| **Arrival** | Group transfer; welcome desk; app-onboarding support | Expedited accreditation; dedicated welcome path | Received by name; accreditation pre-completed; direct to controlled zone |
| **Opening night** | General seating; full participation in polling | Defined seating block; lounge access before and after | Forward seating; concierge zone; discreet routing |
| **Working day** | Roundtable and matchmaking access via app | Curated matchmaking; priority sector-room access | Curated one-to-one; private meeting capability |
| **Social programmes** | Full access to gala and marina dinner | Full access; lounge-adjacent positioning at gala | Dedicated VVIP area at the gala, per the RFP |
| **Close & departure** | Departure information; wave-based transfer | Priority departure handling | Individually managed departure |

**The design test:** at no point should a standard guest experience their tier as an absence. Differentiation is delivered by adding for the senior tiers, never by subtracting from the base experience. A standard invitee at this Summit is still a chosen guest at an invitation-led event.

# 7. Journey Risks

| # | Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The gala transition dissipates the report-launch peak** | **Critical** | Design the transition as a continuous experience; rehearse it |
| 2 | **Arrival experience degrades across a long staggered window** | **High** | Resource arrival for sustained delivery, not a launch peak; consistent staffing across the full window |
| 3 | **App failure or poor adoption breaks the journey's spine** | **High** | The app carries matchmaking, sign-ups, polling and content — early onboarding, offline fallbacks, on-site support |
| 4 | **Day 3 fatigue collapses attendance at the close** | **High** | Resolve departure logistics before the close; build recovery into Day 3 morning; make the close worth staying for |
| 5 | **Guest fragmentation on Day 2** as the group splits across sectors | **Medium-High** | Deliberate regathering moments at brunch and the evening session |
| 6 | **Tiering becomes visible as exclusion** | **Medium-High** | Separate routes rather than visible barriers; never queue senior guests alongside those they outrank |
| 7 | **Heat degrades the experience at daytime touchpoints** | **Medium-High** | Shade, cooling and hydration at every daytime touchpoint; minimise exposed walking |
| 8 | **Departure ends the Summit on a weak note** | **Medium-High** | Designed farewell; holding provision for late flights; departure info issued in advance |
| 9 | **Matchmaking underdelivers** despite being the commercial core | **Medium-High** | Space design, wayfinding and app support treated as one system |
| 10 | **Marina dinner attendance falls away** on a late second night | **Medium** | Simple, short transition; set expectations early in the day |
| 11 | **Pre-arrival communication fragments** across multiple senders | **Medium** | Single owned communication stream |
| 12 | **No post-event follow-through**, leaving commitments unconverted | **Medium** | Clarify ownership with Entlaq; close the journey deliberately |

## Decision Summary

### Decisions Made

| Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **The journey is structured in seven phases**, from invitation to post-event follow-through | The RFP's guest experience does not begin at arrival or end at departure. Phases 1 and 7 are where expectation is set and value is realised. |
| **Six "Moments That Matter" are identified for disproportionate design investment** | Journey quality is not uniform. Concentrating effort on six touchpoints delivers more than spreading it across fifty. |
| **The report launch → gala transition is designated the highest-risk journey moment** | It places a venue change immediately after the Summit's emotional peak. It should be designed as a continuous sequence, not a transport gap. |
| **Tiering is delivered by addition, never subtraction** | A standard invitee is still a chosen guest at an invitation-led event. Differentiation must be invisible to those not receiving it. |
| **The Entlaq app is treated as the journey's single source of truth** | Established by the RFP as the digital layer; physical wayfinding reinforces it rather than competing with it. |
| **Arrival is resourced for sustained delivery, not a peak** | The RFP specifies staggered arrivals; the last guest must receive what the first received. |
| **Departure is treated as a designed farewell** | It is the last impression and the most commonly neglected phase. |

### Decisions Deferred

| Deferred decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Detailed touchpoint choreography per phase** | Requires confirmed venues, timings and the final agenda |
| **Pre-arrival communication design and ownership** | Not addressed in the RFP; ownership undefined |
| **Post-event follow-through** | Not in the RFP's scope of work; may not be the agency's |
| **How tiering is communicated to guests** | Client policy decision with reputational implications |
| **Wellness and workshop participation levels** | Self-selecting; cannot be modelled until sign-ups open |
| **Guest comfort provision at the Day 3 close** | Depends on the Site Inspection Priority assessment in [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md) |

## Open Client Questions

1. **Who owns pre-arrival guest communication** — Entlaq or the agency — and when does it begin?
2. **How is covered vs. uncovered status communicated to guests**, given ~70% are outside the room block?
3. **When will the app be live**, and can matchmaking open before arrival so guests land with meetings already scheduled?
4. **Is post-event follow-through within scope**, or does the journey end at departure?
5. **How should tiering be communicated** — are tiers named to guests, or implied through service?
6. **Do sponsors form a distinct journey track**, or are they absorbed into the VIP and standard tiers?
7. **Is there a designed farewell moment planned**, or does the Summit end with the midday close?
8. **What is expected of the guest during the report launch** — is participation via polling mandatory to the design, and what happens if adoption is partial?
9. **Are there guests with accessibility requirements** affecting journey design?
10. **What follow-up commitment does Entlaq expect from guests**, so the Day 3 close can be designed toward it?
