# Transportation Strategy — ENTLAQ SDR Summit 2026

**Purpose:** Define how ~500 guests, the Entlaq team and the production crew move between arrival points, accommodation and venues across three days.

> **Scope boundary.** This document covers movement demand, fleet strategy, peak management and transport control. It does **not** cover destination context or local transport modes (see [02_destination.md](02_destination.md)), hotel selection (see [04_hotels.md](04_hotels.md)), venue selection or venue logic (see [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md)), the guest's experience of moving (see [07_guest_journey.md](07_guest_journey.md)), or VVIP protocol handling (see [09_vip_experience.md](09_vip_experience.md)).

---

# 1. Objective

Transport is the connective tissue of a multi-venue destination summit. It is invisible when it works and is the only thing anyone remembers when it does not.

**What transport must deliver for this Summit:**

- **Protect the programme.** The RFP places the main stage in the evenings, working sessions in the mornings and social programmes at separate venues. Every one of those transitions is a vehicle movement. If transport slips, the run-of-show slips with it — there is no recovery mechanism once 500 people are in the wrong place.
- **Protect attendance.** Guests do not attend sessions they cannot easily reach. Friction in transport converts directly into empty seats, and the sessions most vulnerable are the ones Entlaq most wants attended.
- **Deliver the tiering.** The RFP funds shuttles *and* cars. That distinction is the physical expression of the VVIP/VIP/standard structure. Ministers arriving on a shared coach is a protocol failure regardless of how good the coach is.
- **Absorb an agenda that is still moving.** The RFP states the agenda will continue to change. Transport must be built to flex, not to a fixed timetable.
- **Serve production as well as guests.** Crew, freight and equipment move on a different schedule from guests — earlier, heavier and often overnight — across the same limited road network.

**Governing principle:** transport should be planned around **peaks, not averages**. The Summit's movement demand is highly concentrated; a fleet sized for average load will fail at every moment that matters.

# 3. Movement Demand Profile

Transport demand on this Summit is not continuous. It is a series of sharp, short peaks separated by long quiet periods.

## 3.1 The five movement types

| Type | Population | Character | Vehicle class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Airport transfers** | Arriving and departing guests | Long window, low intensity, unpredictable timing | Cars for VVIP/VIP; vans and coaches for groups |
| **Hotel ↔ venue shuttles** | Bulk of the ~500 | Short, sharp, high volume, schedule-driven | Coaches on a loop |
| **VVIP and VIP movements** | Ministers, investors, senior speakers | On demand, protocol-sensitive, must never wait | Dedicated cars with assigned drivers |
| **Crew and production movement** | Crew, technical staff | Off-peak, overnight, extended period | Vans and crew transport |
| **Freight and equipment** | Staging, LED, AV, scenic | Pre- and post-event, heavy vehicles | Trucks, forklifts, plant |

**The critical insight:** these five types compete for the same road network at overlapping times, but only two of them — shuttles and VVIP cars — are visible to guests. The other three must be scheduled *around* the guest-facing movements, not alongside them.

## 3.2 Demand across the three days

| Day | Movement pattern | Intensity |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-event** | Crew and freight inbound; speaker and team early arrivals | Sustained, low visibility |
| **Day 1** | All-day staggered arrivals → evening venue call → gala transfer (~300) | **Two guest peaks** |
| **Day 2** | Morning venue call → brunch → rest → evening venue call → late marina transfer | **Four guest peaks — the heaviest day** |
| **Day 3** | Pre-dawn wellness → morning venue call → midday venue call → mass departure | **Four peaks, including the two hardest** |
| **Post-event** | Load-out; crew departures | Sustained, extended |

**Day 2 and Day 3 carry four movement peaks each.** Day 3 is the more difficult of the two, because two of its peaks — the pre-dawn call and the compressed mid-afternoon departure — fall outside normal service hours and cannot be smoothed.

# 4. Fleet Strategy

## 4.1 A three-tier fleet

The RFP's "shuttle + cars" phrasing implies two tiers. In practice, three are required.

| Tier | Purpose | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Dedicated cars** | Ministers, international investors, senior speakers | Assigned vehicle and driver for the duration; never shared; on call rather than scheduled; discreet, high-specification, air-conditioned to standard on arrival |
| **Tier 2 — Executive vans** | VIP guests, small groups, speaker movements, media | Scheduled but flexible; bridges the gap between individual service and mass movement; the workhorse of the fleet |
| **Tier 3 — Shuttle coaches** | Standard invitees and the bulk of guest movement | Fixed-loop, high-frequency, published timings; capacity-led |

**Why three rather than two:** a two-tier model forces every guest who is not VVIP onto a coach. With ~500 guests and a VIP tier containing key founders, C-level executives and media heads, that produces a visible and avoidable service cliff. Tier 2 absorbs it.

## 4.2 Sizing principles

Fleet sizing cannot be finalised until manifests exist, but the governing principles are:

1. **Size to the largest single peak, not the average.** The binding constraints are the Day 1 gala transfer (~300 guests in a short window) and the Day 3 departure compression (~500 guests against airport check-in cut-offs).
2. **Hold reserve capacity.** A fleet at 100% utilisation has no recovery capacity when a vehicle fails or an agenda changes. Reserve should be explicit, not notional.
3. **Assume the fleet is imported, not sourced locally.** Per [02_destination.md](02_destination.md), the dominant local mode is not summit-grade. Vehicles of the required standard must be brought in and managed.
4. **Plan in vehicle-movements, not vehicle-counts.** A coach can run multiple loops; a VVIP car assigned to one principal cannot. The two scale completely differently.
5. **Separate crew transport from guest transport entirely.** Shared vehicles create schedule dependency between two populations with incompatible timings.

# 5. Movement Management by Type

## 5.1 Airport transfers

The arrival window is long and unpredictable — international guests arrive from multiple origins across a wide span, per [02_destination.md](02_destination.md).

**Strategy:** sustained presence over a long operating window rather than surge deployment. A permanent airport reception position for the duration of the arrival window, with vehicles held on standby rather than dispatched per flight. Tier 1 guests receive a named driver and vehicle waiting on arrival; Tier 2 and 3 are consolidated into departures at defined intervals to avoid vehicles running near-empty.

**Open point:** the RFP states transport is provided "between hotels and venues." Whether airport transfers sit inside that scope is undefined and must be confirmed.

## 5.2 Hotel-to-venue shuttles

Guests are distributed across multiple properties per [03_accommodation.md](03_accommodation.md), and venues cluster into two groups per [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md).

**Strategy:** a hub-and-spoke loop rather than point-to-point services. Each guest property is a pickup node; the active venue is the hub. Published, predictable timings — guests tolerate waiting far better than uncertainty. Frequency should increase in the 45 minutes before a session call and immediately after it ends.

**Design consequence:** the number of guest properties directly drives shuttle complexity. Concentrating accommodation reduces transport cost and risk — a point that should feed back into hotel decisions before they are finalised.

## 5.3 VVIP and VIP movement

**Strategy:** dedicated assignment, not dispatch. A minister's vehicle waits for the minister; the minister never waits for a vehicle. Drivers are briefed on routes, venues and access points in advance and remain with their assigned principal throughout.

Routing must align with the VVIP arrival and departure points defined per venue in [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md) — vehicles should discharge directly into controlled zones, never into general circulation. Protocol and security requirements will shape this and are not yet known; see [09_vip_experience.md](09_vip_experience.md).

## 5.4 Crew and freight

**Strategy:** schedule entirely outside guest-facing windows. Load-in, load-out and equipment movements should run overnight or in the early morning, before guest movement begins. Crew transport operates on the build schedule, not the programme schedule, and extends beyond the event dates on both ends.

Freight requires vehicle access, turning space and holding areas at each venue — a venue requirement, defined in [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md), that transport planning must confirm is deliverable.

# 6. Peak Analysis

The seven movements that will determine whether transport succeeds.

| # | Peak | Difficulty | Why it is hard | Strategic response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Day 1 staggered arrivals** | Medium | Long, unpredictable window; guests arrive tired into heat | Sustained presence over hours; standby model rather than per-flight dispatch |
| 2 | **Day 1 gala transfer (~300)** | **High** | Large volume, short window, immediately after the Summit's signature moment; energy must survive the move | Pre-positioned fleet; staged release by tier; treat as a designed transition, not a gap |
| 3 | **Day 2 morning venue call** | Medium | First morning movement; sets the tone for punctuality across the Summit | High frequency in the pre-call window; over-provide on the first morning |
| 4 | **Day 2 late marina transfer** | Medium-High | Late at night, low guest energy, second social venue of the day | Short, simple, frequent; assume attendance is fragile |
| 5 | **Day 3 pre-dawn wellness** | **High** | Outside all normal service hours; sunrise timing is immovable; small numbers, high visibility if it fails | Dedicated small-vehicle provision; driver rostering planned as a separate shift |
| 6 | **Day 3 midday venue call** | Medium-High | Peak heat; guests already fatigued from a dawn start | Minimise exposure between vehicle and venue; vehicles pre-cooled |
| 7 | **Day 3 mass departure** | **Highest** | ~500 guests to a single airport against fixed check-in cut-offs, compressed into "mid-afternoon" per the RFP | Departure waves by flight time, not by hotel; manifest-driven; begin before the close ends for early flights |

**The two hardest peaks are both on Day 3**, at opposite ends of the day. Neither can be smoothed by scheduling — one is fixed by sunrise, the other by flight departure times.

# 7. Transport Control Model

**Single point of control.** With multiple properties, multiple venues and five movement types, transport requires one function that owns the whole picture and can reallocate in real time. Distributed decision-making across venues produces vehicles in the wrong place.

**Live visibility.** The control function needs to know where vehicles are, what has departed and what is inbound. Without it, transport becomes reactive at exactly the moments it must be predictive.

**Guest-facing communication.** Guests need to know where to be and when. Shuttle timings should be published through the Entlaq app — the RFP already establishes an app as the digital layer — supported by physical wayfinding at hotels and venues, which falls within the RFP's signage scope.

**Change absorption.** The RFP states the agenda will keep changing. Transport must have a defined process for absorbing a timing change without re-planning the fleet: buffer capacity, a briefed dispatch function and drivers reachable in real time.

**Escalation authority.** One named person must be able to override the plan — hold a departure, release a reserve vehicle, reroute a VVIP car — without seeking approval. Transport decisions have minutes, not hours.

| # | Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Day 3 departure compression** — ~500 guests to one airport in a narrow window against check-in cut-offs | **Critical** | Manifest-driven departure waves by flight time; begin early departures before the close concludes; confirm airport cut-offs |
| 2 | **Airport transfer scope undefined** — the RFP covers hotel-to-venue transport only | **Critical** | Confirm with Entlaq before fleet sizing; a large scope element may be unowned |
| 3 | **No manifests exist** — arrival and departure timings unknown | **High** | Cannot size the fleet without them; establish a manifest cut-off date |
| 4 | **Gala transfer volume** — ~300 guests in a short window after the signature moment | **High** | Pre-position fleet; staged release; rehearse the transition |
| 5 | **Pre-dawn movement outside service hours** | **High** | Separate driver shift; confirm vehicle availability at that hour; dedicated provision |
| 6 | **Accommodation dispersion multiplies routes** | **High** | Feed transport complexity back into hotel decisions before they are locked |
| 7 | **Fleet must be imported** — local vehicle stock is not summit-grade | **High** | Secure fleet early; confirm supply source and standard |
| 8 | **Agenda instability** — timings will keep changing per the RFP | **Medium-High** | Buffer capacity; flexible dispatch model; avoid fixed published timetables until late |
| 9 | **Single access road** — no alternative route for guests or freight, per [02_destination.md](02_destination.md) | **Medium-High** | Build time buffers into every transfer; monitor conditions |
| 10 | **Transport for uncovered guests undefined** — ~70% of attendees are not in the Entlaq block | **Medium-High** | Clarify whether they are served, and communicate the position early |
| 11 | **Heat exposure at pickup and drop-off points** | **Medium** | Shaded waiting positions; pre-cooled vehicles; minimise exposed walking distance |
| 12 | **Crew and freight movements colliding with guest windows** | **Medium** | Hard scheduling separation; overnight load-in |
| 13 | **Venue access and turning capacity unverified** | **Medium** | Confirm at site inspection alongside venue assessment |

## Decision Summary

### Decisions Made

| Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **A three-tier fleet — dedicated cars, executive vans, shuttle coaches** | The RFP's "shuttle + cars" produces a service cliff for the VIP tier. A middle tier absorbs founders, C-level and media heads without individual car assignment. |
| **Plan to peaks, not averages** | Movement demand is concentrated into ~7 sharp peaks. A fleet sized to average load fails at every moment that matters. |
| **Hub-and-spoke shuttle model** | Guests are distributed across multiple properties; venues cluster into two groups. Loop services are more resilient than point-to-point. |
| **Dedicated assignment for VVIP, not dispatch** | The principal must never wait for a vehicle. This is a protocol requirement, not a service upgrade. |
| **Hard separation of crew and freight from guest movement** | Incompatible schedules; shared vehicles create dependency between populations with different timings. |
| **Single transport control function with escalation authority** | Transport decisions have minutes. Distributed decision-making puts vehicles in the wrong place. |
| **Fleet assumed imported and managed, not locally sourced** | Established in [02_destination.md](02_destination.md) — the dominant local mode is not summit-grade. |

### Decisions Deferred

| Deferred decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Fleet size and vehicle counts** | Require arrival/departure manifests and confirmed guest numbers |
| **Whether airport transfers are in scope** | The RFP covers hotel-to-venue only; ownership undefined |
| **Shuttle routes and timings** | Depend on confirmed hotels and venues |
| **Departure wave structure** | Requires flight manifests and airport check-in cut-offs |
| **VVIP routing at each venue** | Depends on protocol requirements not yet shared |
| **Whether uncovered guests receive transport** | Client policy decision |
