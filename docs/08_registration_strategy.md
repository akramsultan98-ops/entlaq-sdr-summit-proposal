# Registration Strategy — ENTLAQ SDR Summit 2026

**Purpose:** Define how ~500 guests are registered, accredited, badged, onboarded to the Entlaq app, and granted differentiated access across three days and multiple venues.

> **Scope boundary.** This document owns **accreditation, badging, access control and app onboarding** — the mechanics by which a guest becomes a credentialed participant. It does not cover the experiential arc in which registration sits ([07_guest_journey.md](07_guest_journey.md)), the physical registration space and its adjacencies ([05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md)), VVIP protocol handling ([09_vip_experience.md](09_vip_experience.md)), or arrival transport ([06_transportation_strategy.md](06_transportation_strategy.md)).

---

# 1. Objective

Registration is where an invitation becomes an admission. For an invitation-led summit hosting ministers and international investors, it carries three responsibilities that a standard conference check-in does not.

**What registration must deliver:**

- **Control who is present.** The RFP describes an invitation-led event with limited curated public registration. That control is only real if accreditation enforces it. Registration is the security perimeter of the Summit.
- **Enforce the tier structure.** VVIP, VIP and standard tiers carry different access — VIP lounge, VVIP concierge zones, forward seating, matchmaking. Badges are the mechanism that makes tiering operable rather than notional.
- **Onboard the digital layer.** The RFP establishes an Entlaq-controlled app carrying matchmaking, sector-room sign-ups, live polling and content access. A guest who is registered but not onboarded cannot participate in the programme as designed. **App onboarding is not an add-on to registration; it is half of it.**
- **Absorb staggered arrivals.** The RFP explicitly requires registration built for staggered arrival, not a single peak — a fundamentally different operating model.
- **Handle senior guests without process.** A minister cannot be asked to queue, complete a form, or present a document at a desk. Registration must be capable of completing itself invisibly for the top tier.

**Governing principle:** registration should be **complete before the guest arrives**, with the on-site function existing to hand over credentials and resolve exceptions — not to process people.

# 3. Registration Model

## 3.1 Two distinct populations

The RFP describes a hybrid model that produces two populations with different needs.

| Population | Character | Registration implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Invited guests** | Named, pre-selected, tier-assigned | Registration is **confirmation** of a known identity — data already exists |
| **Curated public registrants** | Applied, screened, approved | Registration is **qualification** — an application and approval flow precedes accreditation |

**Strategic consequence:** these cannot share a single process. The invited population needs a fast confirmation path; the curated public population needs an application, review and approval path with a defined acceptance decision. Conflating them creates either an insultingly bureaucratic experience for invitees or an uncontrolled entry route for applicants.

## 3.2 The three-stage model

Registration should be structured so that on-site effort is minimised.

| Stage | When | What happens | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Pre-registration** | Weeks before | Invitation issued and accepted; guest data captured; tier assigned; requirements collected | Establish the accredited list |
| **2 — Pre-arrival** | Days before | Badge produced; app onboarding initiated; access rights assigned; arrival details confirmed | **Complete registration before travel** |
| **3 — On-site** | Day 1 onward | Credential handover; app support; exception handling; late additions | Hand over, don't process |

**The strategic goal is that Stage 3 is a collection point, not a registration desk.** Everything that can be resolved in advance should be. This is what makes staggered arrival manageable and what makes VVIP handling possible at all.

# 4. Pre-Event Registration

**Data capture.** Registration is the only structured opportunity to collect what the rest of the Summit depends on: tier, organisation and role, sector interests for roundtable allocation, dietary and accessibility requirements, arrival and departure details, and consent for app and matchmaking participation. Data not captured here has to be chased later at significantly higher cost.

**Tier assignment.** Every registrant must carry a tier from the outset. Tier drives badge design, access rights, seating, lounge entitlement and hospitality — so late or ambiguous tier assignment cascades into every other workstream.

**Sector alignment.** The SDR spans ten priority sectors and the RFP describes app-driven sector-room sign-ups. Capturing sector interest at registration allows roundtable demand to be modelled *before* the venue configuration is locked — directly relevant to the ten-sectors-versus-six-halls question raised in [05_venue_strategy.md](05_venue_strategy.md).

**Cut-off discipline.** A defined registration cut-off is required, after which additions follow an exception process with named approval authority. Without a cut-off, badge production, catering numbers, seating and access lists cannot be finalised.

# 5. On-Site Accreditation

## 5.1 Operating model for staggered arrivals

The RFP's staggered-arrival requirement inverts the usual design. A conventional registration desk is sized for a morning surge; this one must sustain low-volume throughput across a long window.

**Implications:**

- **Duration over capacity.** Fewer positions, open far longer, consistently staffed. The guest arriving late in the day must receive what the first guest received.
- **Consistency is the hard part.** Sustained delivery over many hours degrades unless staffing is rotated deliberately.
- **Multiple locations may be required.** If guests arrive at different points, accreditation may need to exist in more than one place, or a mobile capability may be needed. Undefined in the RFP.

## 5.2 Differentiated accreditation paths

Not a faster queue — a **separate path**.

| Tier | Path | Model |
|---|---|---|
| **VVIP** | No desk contact | Credentials pre-issued and delivered ahead of arrival or handed over personally in a controlled zone. The guest never encounters a registration process. |
| **VIP** | Dedicated position | A distinct, staffed position with pre-prepared credentials — separate from the general desk, not a priority lane within it. |
| **Standard** | Main accreditation | Well-run, well-staffed, pre-prepared collection against a named list. |
| **Media** | Separate accreditation | Distinct process with its own conditions of access, filming permissions and zone rights. |
| **Sponsors** | Per entitlement | Cannot be defined until sponsor tiers are confirmed — currently **TBD** in the RFP. |
| **Crew & staff** | Separate accreditation | Operates on a different schedule; issued before guest arrival begins. |

## 5.3 Exception handling

Every summit generates exceptions: name errors, unannounced delegation members, late substitutions, lost badges, senior guests arriving unlisted. **The exception path is where registration is judged**, because it is precisely where the most sensitive cases surface. It requires a discreet position away from the main desk, staff empowered to make decisions, on-site badge production, and a clear escalation route to someone who can authorise an unlisted senior guest without delay.

# 6. Badging & Access Control

## 6.1 What the badge must do

The badge is the physical instrument of the tier structure. It performs four functions: **identification** (who this is), **authorisation** (where they may go), **differentiation** (what tier they hold), and **facilitation** (helping guests identify each other for networking — the Summit's commercial purpose).

**Design tension to resolve:** the badge must be legible enough to enable networking and access control, but tiering must not read as a visible hierarchy. Per [07_guest_journey.md](07_guest_journey.md), a standard guest should never experience their tier as a deficiency. The recommended approach is that **badges carry clear identity and subtle tier encoding** — differentiation that staff read instantly and guests do not read as a ranking.

## 6.2 Access zones

Access control is required wherever the RFP defines a restricted space:

| Zone | Access | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| General Summit areas | All accredited | Baseline |
| Main stage — forward/VVIP seating | VVIP, defined VIP | RFP tiering |
| VIP lounge | VIP and VVIP | RFP requires fit-out |
| VVIP concierge / security-adjacent zone | VVIP + authorised staff | RFP explicit |
| Backstage and production areas | Crew and authorised only | Operational |
| Gala Dinner | ~300 of ~500 — **a subset** | RFP: gala is ~300 |
| Gala VVIP area | VVIP only | RFP explicit |
| Media positions | Accredited media | Operational |

**The gala is the sharpest access-control problem in the Summit.** The RFP specifies ~300 guests at the gala against ~500 at the Summit. That means **roughly 200 accredited guests are not invited to the Day 1 gala** — a differentiation that must be established at registration, encoded on the badge, communicated to guests in advance, and enforced at the door. If this is discovered on the night, it becomes a visible and personal exclusion at the Summit's premium social moment. **This needs resolving with Entlaq early.**

## 6.3 Technology

Badge technology is undefined in the RFP. The choice sits between simple printed badges with visual checking, and scannable credentials enabling session tracking, attendance data and app integration. The decision should follow from what Entlaq wants to *know*: if attendance analytics or app-linked access matter, that must be specified now, as it affects badge production, scanning infrastructure and staffing.

# 7. App Onboarding

The RFP makes the app central: matchmaking, sector-room sign-ups, live polling and content access all run through it. **Every one of those is a programme function, not a convenience.** A guest without the app cannot participate in the report launch's live polling, cannot sign up for sector rooms, and cannot access matchmaking — the Summit's commercial core.

**This makes app adoption a registration responsibility, not an IT one.**

**Strategy:**

- **Onboard before arrival.** Guests should arrive with the app installed and matchmaking already underway — maximising the value of limited time on site and removing a Day 1 bottleneck.
- **Staff the on-site stations properly.** The RFP requires app-onboarding stations. These need people capable of resolving installation and login problems, not a signpost.
- **Assume partial adoption and design for it.** A proportion of guests will arrive without the app or unable to use it. Live polling at the report launch must degrade gracefully rather than exclude, and sector sign-ups need a non-app fallback.
- **Support senior guests differently.** Ministers and senior investors are the least likely to self-onboard and the most consequential to exclude. Assisted onboarding should be built into VVIP handling rather than assumed.

**Ownership note:** the app is Entlaq-controlled. Responsibility for its readiness, functionality and support sits with Entlaq, but registration depends on it entirely. This interface must be defined.

# 8. Data & Governance

**Single authoritative list.** One registration database with defined ownership and controlled update rights. Multiple versions across teams is the most common failure mode in accreditation.

**Data protection.** The registration list contains named senior government and business figures with travel and personal details — a genuinely sensitive dataset. Handling, storage, access rights and retention need to be defined, and requirements may vary by guest nationality. **Not addressed in the RFP.**

**Confidentiality of the guest list.** Who is attending is itself sensitive information at a summit of this profile. Access to the full list should be restricted, and the badge system should not expose the attendee list to general view.

**Integration.** Registration data drives accommodation rooming, transport manifests, catering numbers, seating and app profiles. Whether these integrate or are reconciled manually is a decision that should be made deliberately.

## Decision Summary

### Decisions Made

| Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **Registration is structured in three stages, with on-site as handover rather than processing** | The only way to make staggered arrival manageable and VVIP handling possible. Everything resolvable in advance should be. |
| **Invited guests and curated public registrants follow separate processes** | They require confirmation and qualification respectively; a single flow serves neither. |
| **Differentiated accreditation paths, not priority queues** | A separate path is the only model in which a minister never encounters a registration process. |
| **App onboarding is treated as half of registration, not an adjunct** | Matchmaking, sector sign-ups and live polling are programme functions. A guest without the app cannot participate as designed. |
| **Badges carry clear identity with subtle tier encoding** | Reconciles the need for access control and networking with the principle that tiering must not read as visible hierarchy. |
| **Gala access is designated a registration problem, not a door problem** | ~200 accredited guests are not gala invitees. This must be established, encoded and communicated well in advance. |
| **A single authoritative registration database with controlled update rights** | Version conflict is the most common accreditation failure mode. |
| **An empowered exception path with named escalation authority** | Exceptions are where the most sensitive cases surface and where registration is judged. |

### Decisions Deferred

| Deferred decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Badge technology and whether access is scanned** | Depends on what data Entlaq wants to capture |
| **Sponsor accreditation model** | Sponsor tiers and entitlements are TBD in the RFP |
| **Number and location of registration positions** | Requires confirmed venues and arrival profile |
| **Curated public registration criteria and process** | Client policy decision |
| **Data protection framework** | Requires legal position and guest nationality profile |
| **The basis on which gala invitations are determined** | Client decision with reputational implications |
| **Whether registration systems integrate with accommodation, transport and catering** | Depends on system choices not yet made |

## Open Client Questions

1. **Who owns the guest database and the registration platform** — Entlaq, the agency, or a third-party system?
2. **On what basis are the ~300 gala invitations determined**, and when will guests be told? Roughly 200 accredited guests are not invited, and this must be handled before the night.
3. **What is the curated public registration process** — how do applicants apply, who screens them, and who approves?
4. **When will the app be ready**, and can onboarding begin before arrival?
5. **What does Entlaq want to know from registration data** — is session-level attendance tracking required, which would drive scannable badges?
6. **Who is responsible for app support** during the Summit — Entlaq or the agency?
7. **What are the data protection requirements** for a guest list containing senior government figures, and are there nationality-specific obligations?
8. **What are the sponsor tiers and entitlements**, and what accreditation rights do they carry?
9. **How many media are expected**, and what access conditions apply to them?
10. **Is badge design and production within the agency's scope?** The RFP states "design and/or production to be agreed."
11. **What is the registration cut-off date**, and who may authorise additions afterwards?
12. **Will guests arrive at a single point or several**, determining whether registration operates in more than one location?
