# 1. Objective

Accommodation is the only element of a destination summit that touches every guest, on every day, outside the programme. For a three-day, invitation-led summit built around ministers, international investors and senior founders, it carries strategic weight well beyond providing a bed.

**What accommodation has to deliver for this Summit:**

- **It sets the tone before the programme starts.** The first physical impression of the Summit is not the main stage — it is arrival and check-in. The RFP's stated ambition is a *premium, invitation-led experience* that positions Egypt as a serious destination for founders and capital. A slow, disorganised or mismatched check-in undermines that positioning before a single session opens.
- **It protects the seniority hierarchy.** The RFP defines three distinct tiers (VVIP, VIP, standard). Accommodation is where tiering is most visible and most consequential. Placing a minister or an international investor in the wrong standard of room is a diplomatic and commercial problem, not a logistical one.
- **It underwrites guest energy across three days.** The programme runs late evenings and starts early — the RFP places main-stage programmes in the evenings, an early-morning wellness activation on Day 3, and working sessions in the mornings. Rest quality directly determines attendance and engagement in the sessions Entlaq most wants attended.
- **It is the base for the informal programme.** Much of the value of a summit like this happens between sessions. Where guests sleep determines who they run into at breakfast, which materially affects the matchmaking and deal-making outcomes the RFP is designed around.
- **It carries the highest-consequence failure modes.** Almost every other production issue can be recovered on the day. An overbooked property, a missing VVIP reservation, or a lost rooming list on arrival day cannot.

**Strategic principle for this document:** accommodation should be planned as a **tiered guest-experience system**, not as a room-booking exercise. Rooms are allocated to protect outcomes — ministerial comfort and privacy, investor accessibility, speaker readiness, and crew functionality — in that order of consequence.

# 3. Accommodation Categories

Recommended accommodation strategy per group. **No hotels are assigned at this stage** — this defines the standard each group requires so that properties can be matched to it once the block is known.

Categories are ordered by **allocation priority**: when inventory is constrained, higher-priority groups are protected first.

---

### 3.1 Ministers *(RFP tier: VVIP)*

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Highest — Priority 1.** Protected absolutely; never displaced, never downgraded, never consolidated to release inventory. |
| **Room Standard** | Top available tier — suite or equivalent. The standard must be defensible in protocol terms, not merely comfortable. Parity across ministers matters more than absolute level: no visible hierarchy between peers of equal rank. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Maximum.** Separated floor or wing, controlled access, no shared circulation with general attendees. Guest identity should not be discoverable through the hotel. Delegation staff and security accommodated adjacently. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | Fully pre-completed. No queue, no desk, no paperwork on arrival — room assigned, keys issued, formalities settled in advance. Received by a named senior host, not by general staff. |
| **Special Requirements** | Protocol and security coordination; accompanying delegation and aide rooms; discreet arrival and departure routing; flexible meal timing outside standard service hours; capacity for private meetings without using public space; short-notice schedule changes must be absorbable. Ministerial attendance is often confirmed late and can change — inventory must be held accordingly. |

---

### 3.2 International Investors *(RFP tier: VVIP)*

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 1.** The RFP covers their flights, signalling that this group is a primary investment. Accommodation should match that commitment. |
| **Room Standard** | High-end — premium room or junior suite. This group travels internationally at a high standard and will benchmark against it. Consistency across the group is important; visible disparity between investors is a commercial risk. |
| **Privacy Level** | **High.** Discreet, quiet, insulated from general attendee traffic — but *deliberately less isolated than ministers*. This group is at the Summit to be met, so accommodation should support accessibility, not seclusion. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | Pre-assigned and expedited. Arrivals will be long-haul, jet-lagged and time-zone-scattered. Room readiness must be guaranteed regardless of arrival hour, including well outside standard check-in windows. |
| **Special Requirements** | Reliable high-grade connectivity for calls across time zones; late and early room access; in-room work space; quiet rooms away from the late-night programme footprint; dietary and cultural provisions; discretion about who is staying where. Co-location with matchmaking counterparts is a strategic advantage worth planning for. |

---

### 3.3 Speakers

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 2 — but with Priority 1 treatment on timing.** Speakers are programme-critical: a speaker who cannot be located or is not rested is a live show risk. |
| **Room Standard** | Premium standard. Comfortable and credible, but the differentiator is **function over luxury** — quiet, well-lit, workable. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Moderate to high.** Enough seclusion to prepare undisturbed, but speakers should remain reachable by the production team at all times. Contactability outranks privacy for this group. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | Priority and often **earliest** — many speakers arrive ahead of the general population for rehearsals and technical checks. Early check-in should be treated as a default assumption, not an exception. Speaker rooms should be confirmed before any other category, since a speaker room failure has immediate programme consequences. |
| **Special Requirements** | Proximity clustering so the production team can reach the whole group quickly; quiet rooms suitable for rehearsal and content review; reliable connectivity for remote prep; tolerance for late-changing arrival times; some speakers will hold VVIP or VIP status independently and must be treated at the higher of the two standards. Speakers who are also ministers or investors follow the higher category. |

---

### 3.4 VIP Guests *(RFP tier: VIP — key founders, C-level, media heads)*

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 2.** The core of the Summit's commercial and ecosystem value. |
| **Room Standard** | Consistent upper-mid to premium standard. **Uniformity is the governing principle** — this is a peer group that talks to each other, and visible inconsistency within the tier creates friction disproportionate to the actual difference. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Moderate.** Standard privacy expectations. This group benefits from proximity and visibility to one another; over-isolating them works against the Summit's networking purpose. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | Expedited but not individually escorted. A dedicated fast lane rather than a personal host. Pre-assignment strongly recommended given the staggered arrival pattern the RFP describes. |
| **Special Requirements** | Deliberate co-location to maximise informal encounter — the shared breakfast and lobby environment is a genuine programme asset for this tier; dietary requirements captured in advance; late-night access given the evening programme; clear, self-service information so the group is not dependent on staff for routine questions. |

---

### 3.5 Sponsors

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 3 — but contractually driven and potentially escalating.** The RFP states sponsor tiers and entitlements are **TBD**. Room entitlements may be embedded in sponsorship agreements, which would convert this into a contractual obligation rather than a discretionary allocation. |
| **Room Standard** | Tiered to sponsorship level once tiers are defined. Until then, plan a **premium baseline** for headline sponsors and a standard baseline for lower tiers. Sponsor standard should not visibly exceed VIP guest standard. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Standard.** Sponsors generally want visibility and access, not seclusion. Accessibility to the guest population is usually the entitlement they actually value. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | Standard to expedited depending on tier. Sponsor arrivals often include staff and materials, so a slightly wider arrival window should be assumed. |
| **Special Requirements** | **This group carries the highest specification uncertainty in the entire block.** Room entitlements must be reconciled against signed sponsorship agreements before allocation is finalised. Sponsor delegations tend to grow late. Recommendation: hold a defined sponsor sub-block and release it only against confirmed contractual entitlements. |

---

### 3.6 Media

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 3 — with a Priority 2 carve-out.** The RFP places *media heads* in the VIP tier, so media is not a single group. Senior media leadership follows the VIP standard; working press and crew follow the media standard below. |
| **Room Standard** | Functional, comfortable standard rooms. The operative requirement is **working capability**, not luxury. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Low to moderate.** Media benefit from being close to the action and to each other. Isolation is counterproductive. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | Standard, but with **flexible timing** — media schedules follow the news cycle and the programme, including very late finishes after the evening sessions. Late-night check-in capability should be assumed. |
| **Special Requirements** | Strong, dependable connectivity is the single most important factor for this group — filing deadlines depend on it; workable in-room desk space; tolerance for irregular hours; clustering to simplify coordination and briefings. Media should be housed separately from ministers and international investors to protect VVIP privacy — **this is a deliberate separation, not an oversight**. |

---

### 3.7 Entlaq Team

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 2 for leadership, Priority 4 for wider team.** The RFP explicitly includes "team" within the ~150 rooms, so this is a funded category, not an afterthought. |
| **Room Standard** | Standard rooms for the team; premium for Entlaq leadership, who will be hosting VVIPs directly and may need to receive guests. Twin-sharing is acceptable for junior team members if inventory is constrained. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Low.** Functional. Team accommodation should prioritise proximity to guests over comfort or seclusion. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | **Earliest — ahead of all guest categories.** The team must be in place and operational before the first guest arrives. Team rooms should be confirmed and occupied on the day before guest arrivals begin. |
| **Special Requirements** | Deliberate distribution: senior Entlaq hosts should be housed **in the same properties as the VVIP and VIP guests they are responsible for**, so response times are short. Some team members will keep irregular hours and need rooms placed where late movement causes no disturbance. A small number of team rooms should be positioned to double as informal working space. |

---

### 3.8 Production Crew

| Dimension | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Priority** | **Priority 4 for standard, Priority 1 for certainty.** Crew rooms can be the most basic in the block, but they are the least negotiable in terms of *existence* — an unaccommodated crew stops the show. |
| **Room Standard** | Functional standard rooms. **Twin-sharing is the assumed default** for crew and should be planned as such. |
| **Privacy Level** | **Minimal.** Not a consideration for this group. |
| **Check-in Requirements** | **Earliest of all — significantly ahead of the Summit.** Crew arrive for load-in and build well before Day 1 and depart after load-out completes, so their occupancy period is **longer than the Summit itself and extends outside the event dates on both ends**. This is a structurally different booking pattern from every other group. |
| **Special Requirements** | Rooms must support inverted schedules — crew will sleep during the day and work overnight during build. Housing crew away from guest properties is strongly recommended, both to protect the guest experience and to give crew usable rest conditions. Room release must not be tied to the guest departure date. **Critical open point:** it is unclear whether crew accommodation is intended to sit inside the ~150-room block or outside it — see Section 7. |

# 4. Room Allocation Strategy

> **This is a recommendation, not a plan.** The RFP states ~150 rooms without any breakdown. The distribution below is a planning baseline built on the tier structure the RFP defines. **It is provisional until final attendee numbers, the covered-guest list, sponsor entitlements and crew headcount are confirmed by Entlaq.** Every figure is expected to move.

### Recommended baseline distribution

| Category | Rooms | Share | Rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Ministers & government delegations | 12 | 8% | Small headcount, high consequence. Includes aide and delegation rooms. |
| International Investors | 30 | 20% | RFP covers their flights — the largest single funded guest commitment. |
| Speakers | 22 | 15% | Programme-critical across three days and multiple session formats. |
| VIP Guests (founders, C-level, media heads) | 20 | 13% | Core commercial audience; deliberately capped as most VIPs are not covered. |
| Sponsors | 14 | 9% | Held against entitlements that are currently **TBD**. |
| Media | 10 | 7% | Working press; senior media heads counted under VIP. |
| Entlaq Team | 18 | 12% | Explicitly included in the RFP's room block. |
| Production Crew | 16 | 11% | Assumes twin-sharing; longer occupancy than the event. |
| **Unallocated contingency** | **8** | **5%** | Held back — see below. |
| **Total** | **150** | **100%** | |

### Principles behind the distribution

1. **The block covers a minority of the audience.** ~150 rooms against ~500 guests means roughly **70% of attendees are not accommodated by Entlaq**. The strategy must therefore be explicit about who is covered and who arranges their own stay — this is a communications requirement as much as a logistical one, and the criteria need to be defined early and applied consistently.
2. **Protect the top tier absolutely.** Ministers and international investors together take ~28% of the block for a small fraction of the headcount. This is intentional: consequence, not volume, drives allocation at the top.
3. **Hold a contingency.** The ~5% unallocated buffer is deliberate. Late ministerial confirmations, speaker additions, sponsor entitlements and VVIP delegation growth are near-certain on an event of this profile. A block with no slack will fail.
4. **Team and crew are ~23% combined.** This is a substantial share of a guest-facing block and should be validated early — if crew can sit outside the 150, roughly 16 rooms return to guest use, which is a material change to guest coverage.
5. **Distribute by group, not by convenience.** The RFP confirms multiple properties. Categories should be kept coherent across properties — splitting a peer group across hotels creates visible inequality and coordination cost.
6. **Night-count is not uniform.** Guests need ~2–3 nights; speakers and team need more; crew need substantially more. Total room-nights will therefore significantly exceed 150 × 3, and the block should be modelled in room-nights rather than rooms.

# 5. Operational Considerations

Strategic requirements that the accommodation approach must accommodate. These define *what must be true*, not the procedures to achieve it.

**Early arrivals.** The RFP places arrivals on Day 1 daytime, but speakers, team and crew all need to be in place before that, and international arrivals will land outside standard check-in windows. The strategy must assume **a meaningful volume of pre-event-date occupancy** and guarantee room readiness independent of standard hotel check-in times. This is a block-structure decision made at contracting, not a request made on the day.

**Late departures.** Day 3 ends with departures by mid-afternoon, but international outbound connections will not align neatly with that. The strategy must provide for **late checkout and a post-checkout holding provision** for guests whose departure falls after the block releases — particularly international investors and ministers, for whom being turned out of a room is an unacceptable outcome.

**Hospitality desk.** With guests spread across multiple properties, a **single accountable point of ownership** for accommodation is essential — a defined hospitality function that owns the rooming picture across all properties, rather than reliance on each hotel's own front desk. Without it, no one holds the complete picture, and cross-property issues have no owner.

**VIP check-in.** VVIP and VIP check-in must be **decoupled from the standard hotel process entirely**. The strategic requirement is that senior guests never encounter a queue, a form, or a payment discussion. This implies pre-settled arrangements and pre-assigned rooms as a structural precondition, not a service upgrade.

**Welcome packs.** In-room welcome materials are the first branded touchpoint a guest encounters and should be treated as part of the Summit's creative system rather than a hotel amenity. Contents should be **tiered to match guest category**. Distribution across multiple properties needs to be planned as a coordinated delivery, and packs must be in place before the first arrivals.

**Rooming list management.** With multiple properties and multiple guest categories, the rooming list is the **single highest-risk document** in the accommodation workstream. It requires one authoritative version with clear ownership, controlled update rights, and a defined cut-off after which changes follow an exception process. Version conflicts across properties are the most common failure mode in multi-hotel events.

**Last-minute changes.** Late changes are certain, not possible: ministerial attendance confirms late, investor travel shifts, speaker line-ups move, and sponsor delegations grow. The strategy must be **change-tolerant by design** — held contingency, defined escalation authority, and agreed rules for who can be moved and who cannot. Ministers and international investors should be structurally exempt from reallocation.

**Speaker arrivals.** Speakers arrive on the least predictable schedule and have the highest programme consequence. Their accommodation must be **confirmed earliest and held firmest**, with arrival timing tracked against rehearsal and technical-check commitments. Speaker rooming should be treated as part of programme readiness, not general hospitality.

# 6. Risks

Accommodation-related risks only.

| # | Risk | Impact | Mitigation strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ownership of accommodation is undefined.** The RFP's Scope of Work does not include accommodation, yet the room block is stated. If neither party owns it, it goes unmanaged. | Critical | Resolve scope ownership before any further planning — first item in Section 7. |
| 2 | **Coverage gap communicated late.** ~70% of the ~500 guests are not covered by the block. If covered/uncovered status is unclear or announced late, uncovered senior guests will assume they are covered. | High | Define coverage criteria early; communicate status explicitly at invitation stage. |
| 3 | **September availability pressure.** *External Research:* September is a busy period in El Gouna, and the block spans multiple properties. Inventory may not be securable at the required standard if held late. | High | Secure and contract the block as early as possible; confirm standards at contracting. |
| 4 | **Late VVIP confirmations exceed held inventory.** Ministerial and delegation attendance typically confirms very late and grows. | High | Maintain the unallocated contingency; hold VVIP-standard rooms specifically, not generic rooms. |
| 5 | **Sponsor entitlements are undefined (RFP: TBD).** Contractual room obligations may emerge after allocation is set. | High | Ring-fence a sponsor sub-block; reconcile against signed agreements before finalising. |
| 6 | **Rooming-list fragmentation across properties.** Multiple hotels, multiple categories, multiple update sources. | High | Single authoritative list, single owner, controlled change process, defined cut-off. |
| 7 | **Standard inconsistency within a tier.** Multi-property blocks make it difficult to hold one standard across a peer group. | Medium-High | Benchmark standards per tier at contracting; keep peer groups property-coherent. |
| 8 | **Crew rooms displacing guest rooms.** If crew sits inside the 150, guest coverage shrinks materially — and the crew occupancy period extends beyond the event on both ends. | Medium-High | Confirm whether crew is inside or outside the block; contract crew nights separately. |
| 9 | **Arrival/departure timing mismatch.** International schedules will not align with standard check-in and checkout, and Day 3 departures compress into a narrow window. | Medium-High | Contract early check-in and late checkout provisions into the block terms upfront. |
| 10 | **VVIP privacy compromised by co-location.** Housing media or general attendees alongside ministers or investors exposes VVIPs to unwanted contact. | Medium-High | Enforce deliberate separation of media and VVIP properties or floors. |
| 11 | **Guest-experience inconsistency across properties.** Different hotels deliver different service levels for guests nominally in the same tier. | Medium | Set a minimum service standard across all block properties; brief each property to it. |
| 12 | **Night-count underestimation.** Modelling 150 rooms rather than room-nights understates the true requirement given extended speaker, team and crew stays. | Medium | Model the block in room-nights from the outset. |
| 13 | **Late cancellations and attrition liability.** Blocks held against uncertain attendance carry release-deadline exposure. | Medium | Agree release deadlines and attrition terms at contracting. |
| 14 | **Accessibility requirements identified late.** Accessible rooms are limited and cannot be created at short notice. | Medium | Capture accessibility needs at invitation stage, not at rooming-list stage. |
| 15 | **Programme noise affecting rest.** Late-night programming on Days 1 and 2 against early Day 3 and morning working sessions. | Medium | Place noise-sensitive categories away from late-activity footprints. |

# 7. Client Clarifications

Questions to put to Entlaq before any hotel recommendation is made. Ordered by how much each one blocks the next stage of work.

### Blocking — cannot proceed to hotel recommendations without these

1. **Is accommodation within the agency's scope of work?** The RFP's Scope of Work (4.1–4.6) does not include it, but the room block is stated. Is the agency expected to source, contract and manage the block, to manage it against Entlaq-contracted inventory, or not at all?
2. **Has any room inventory already been held or contracted?** If so, at which properties, at what standard, for which dates, and under what release terms?
3. **Who owns the rooming list and the relationship with each property** — Entlaq, the agency, or a third-party partner?
4. **What is the definitive breakdown of the ~150 rooms** across guest categories, if one exists? If not, is the recommendation in Section 4 an acceptable planning baseline?
5. **Who exactly is a "covered guest"?** What criteria determine whether an attendee receives accommodation, and has that been communicated to invitees yet?
6. **Are the ~150 rooms inclusive of production crew**, or does crew accommodation sit outside the block?

### High priority — needed to finalise the allocation

7. **What is the expected headcount per category** — ministers, delegations, international investors, speakers, VIP guests, sponsors, media, Entlaq team, crew?
8. **How many nights per category?** Are guests two nights or three, and what is the assumed arrival/departure pattern per group?
9. **What are the sponsor room entitlements?** The RFP marks tiers as TBD — are any room commitments already contractually promised?
10. **What is the required room standard per tier**, and is there an existing benchmark or precedent from previous Entlaq events?
11. **How many ministers and government delegations are expected**, and what is the accompanying delegation size per minister?
12. **Are there protocol or security requirements** attached to any VVIP that affect property selection, floor allocation or access control?
13. **How many properties are envisaged**, and is there a preference for concentrating the block or spreading it?
14. **Is a single host property required** for VVIPs, or is a distributed model acceptable?

### Required before finalising operational planning

15. **What is the expected arrival window on Day 1**, and how many guests arrive before that day?
16. **What are the confirmed departure patterns on Day 3**, given the RFP's mid-afternoon farewell?
17. **Are early check-in and late checkout to be contracted as standard**, and for which categories?
18. **Who funds and produces welcome packs**, and should contents be tiered by category?
19. **Will the Entlaq event app carry accommodation information** for guests, and if so, who maintains that content?
20. **Who staffs the hospitality function**, and is it expected to cover all properties or only the primary one?
21. **What is the change-authority process** — who can approve a room change or upgrade once the list is locked, and by when must changes be submitted?
22. **Have accessibility or medical requirements been captured** for any confirmed attendee?
23. **Are there dietary, cultural or religious provisions** to be reflected in property selection?
24. **What are the accommodation arrangements for the ~70% of guests not covered** — is a preferred-rate arrangement or a recommended-property list expected, and does the agency have any role in it?
