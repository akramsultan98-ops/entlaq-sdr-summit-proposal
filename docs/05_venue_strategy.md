# Venue Strategy — ENTLAQ SDR Summit 2026

**Purpose:** Define why each function of the Summit should take place in a specific *type* of venue. This is not a venue list and not a booking document — it is the logic that should govern venue selection once the site inspection confirms what is actually available.

> **Source discipline:** The RFP is the primary source throughout. Section 2 contains only RFP-stated facts. Where external information is used — principally in Section 4 — it is labelled **External Research** and requires verification at the site inspection.

---

# 1. Objective

In a single-venue conference, venue strategy is a floor-plan exercise. In a multi-day destination summit spread across separate sites, it is the structural decision that determines whether the programme works at all.

**What venue strategy has to resolve for this Summit:**

- **It converts a three-act narrative into physical space.** The RFP's design logic is *Diagnose → Dive → Do*. Each act has a different mode — a headline reveal, sector-level working sessions, then commitments. These modes need genuinely different rooms. A space that delivers a data-wall reveal to 500 people is the wrong space for a 20-person sector roundtable, and vice versa. Venue strategy is what stops the programme being flattened into whatever the available room can hold.
- **It governs the rhythm of the day.** The RFP is explicit: evenings host the main stage, mornings host smaller sessions in air-conditioned venues, middays are for networking and leisure. That rhythm is a *climate and energy* response as much as a programming one. Venue selection either supports it or fights it.
- **It determines how many times guests must move.** Every venue change costs time, attention and attendance. Each transition is a point where a programme can slip and where guests can be lost. The strategic goal is to minimise transitions without collapsing distinct functions into unsuitable shared space.
- **It sets the production burden.** Separate venues mean separate load-ins, separate technical infrastructure, separate crews and separate rehearsals. Venue count is the single largest driver of production complexity and risk on this project.
- **It protects the tiering.** The RFP defines VVIP, VIP and standard tiers. Venue layout — lounges, concierge zones, arrival routes, sightlines — is where tiering is delivered or where it visibly fails.
- **It carries the highest-consequence single moment.** The report launch is described as *the* signature moment. Everything about the main-stage venue must serve that one reveal.

**Governing principle:** venues should be selected against the *function* they must perform, not against their appeal as locations. Where a candidate venue and a required function conflict, the function wins — or the function moves.

## 4.1 SIA

**RFP status:** Confirmed main venue — evening programmes, report launch, Day 3 midday close.

**External Research:** SÍA is an **outdoor events venue and concept by The Smokery, overlooking the Red Sea in El Gouna**. It operates as an events and celebrations venue. **No published capacity, technical specification or address is available** on its own channels or on El Gouna's official site.

| Assessment | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Fit for evening main stage** | **Strong in principle.** An outdoor Red Sea setting after dark is genuinely distinctive and gives the report launch a sense of occasion no indoor room would match. Darkness also favours large-format LED — the data-wall will read far better at night than in daylight. |
| **Fit for the Day 3 midday close** | **Site Inspection Priority — see below.** The RFP places the Day 3 close at SIA at midday. Because SIA is an outdoor venue, this session sits in different conditions from the Day 1 and Day 2 evening programmes: peak September daytime heat, and daylight rather than darkness behind a large-format LED. **No recommendation is made here.** The conditions must be assessed on site before any position is taken. |
| **Capacity** | **Unknown and unverifiable publicly.** Whether SIA can seat ~500 with clear data-wall sightlines is the first thing to establish at site inspection. |
| **Technical** | As an outdoor venue: wind loading on large-format LED and rigging is a primary engineering constraint; power capacity is unknown and likely requires generator support; no permanent AV infrastructure should be assumed. |
| **Ancillary space** | Backstage, speaker ready room, media area and VIP lounge availability at SIA are unknown. If SIA cannot host them, they must be built as temporary structures — a material addition to scope. |
| **Verdict** | **Retain as main venue, with two conditions:** confirm capacity and sightlines at inspection, and complete the Day 3 midday assessment below. |

### Site Inspection Priority — Day 3 Midday Closing Session

The Day 3 close is the only main-stage session scheduled outside evening hours. It is therefore the only one whose conditions cannot be inferred from the Day 1 and Day 2 programmes, and the only one this document deliberately declines to make a recommendation on.

**No position should be taken on this session — including any change of venue, timing or format — until it has been assessed on site.** The site inspection should evaluate the following six criteria at SIA, at the actual time of day the session is scheduled:

| # | Criterion | What to establish on site |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Guest comfort** | Whether ~500 senior guests can sit through a full session at midday in the venue's real conditions — seating, airflow, surface temperature, and how long the session can credibly run |
| 2 | **Heat exposure** | Actual measured conditions at SIA at the scheduled hour in September, including direct sun angle across the seating footprint and any cooling effect from the Red Sea aspect |
| 3 | **Shade availability** | What permanent shade exists, what the venue can provide, and what temporary shade structures the site can physically accommodate over an audience of this size |
| 4 | **LED screen visibility in daylight** | Whether the data-wall remains legible from every seat in direct daylight, at what brightness specification, and whether shading or repositioning the screen changes the answer |
| 5 | **Technical production feasibility** | Whether the main-stage build serving the Day 1 and Day 2 evening programmes can serve this session unchanged, or whether daytime use requires a different technical configuration |
| 6 | **Audience experience** | Whether the session can deliver the intended sense of occasion in daylight — the close may carry announcements, and the venue's atmospheric strengths after dark do not automatically transfer to midday |

**Why this is being handled as an inspection priority rather than a recommendation:** the variables above are physical and site-specific. Shade provision, screen specification and the venue's real midday microclimate cannot be established from public sources, and SIA publishes no technical data at all. A recommendation made now would rest on assumption rather than observation. Once the six criteria are assessed, a properly grounded recommendation can be made — whether that is retaining the session as scheduled with specified mitigations, or proposing an alternative for Entlaq's consideration.

## 4.2 TU Berlin El Gouna Campus

**RFP status:** Named as the likely roundtable venue — "a campus setting such as the TU Berlin El Gouna campus or another El Gouna school venue (final venue to be confirmed)."

**External Research:** The campus extends over approximately **10,000 m² of usable area**, comprising large lecture halls, a library, an auditorium and seven further buildings with seminar rooms, offices and laboratories.

| Facility | Detail *(External Research)* |
|---|---|
| **Auditorium** | **400 seats**, with projection, audio, video and sound systems; gooseneck microphone and multimedia touch panel; **integrated discussion and interpreter system** with video-conferencing capability |
| **Lecture / meeting halls** | **6 halls for up to 50 guests each**, ground floor; **flexible divider walls between pairs allow up to 120** |
| **AV provision** | Audio-visual presentation facilities in 5 halls; audio only in others |
| **Climate control** | **All meeting halls and rooms have independent climate control** |
| **Overall flexibility** | Indoor and outdoor facilities, capacity **10 to 400 guests** |
| **Outdoor** | Three open-air landscaped areas; main event court **shaded, 150–200 capacity** |

| Assessment | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Fit for roundtables** | **Very strong — the best-matched venue in the Summit.** Independent climate control directly satisfies the RFP's air-conditioning requirement. Six halls with divider walls give real configuration flexibility. Existing AV in five halls reduces technical build. |
| **Capacity tension** | **The SDR spans ten priority sectors, but the campus has six halls.** If every sector needs a simultaneous roundtable, the campus cannot host them in one wave. Either roundtables run in two waves, sectors are combined, or additional rooms are found. **This is a programming decision that must be made before venue confirmation.** |
| **Fit for workshops** | **Strong.** The same room stock serves Day 3 workshops, which run on a different day — efficient reuse with no clash. |
| **Fit for matchmaking** | **Good.** Seminar rooms and circulation space suit semi-private 1:1s. The shaded outdoor event court (150–200) is a credible break and activation zone. |
| **Fit as an alternative main stage** | **Not proposed as a main-stage alternative.** The 400-seat auditorium is short of ~500 and is a fixed academic room rather than a stage-build environment, so it cannot replicate the report launch. Its indoor, climate-controlled, AV-equipped characteristics are noted here as **factual context only** — they may become relevant depending on the outcome of the Day 3 site inspection assessment, but no recommendation is made ahead of that assessment. |
| **Limitations** | An academic environment, not a premium one — significant venue dressing needed to meet the Summit's positioning. Availability during the university term is unconfirmed. Ancillary VIP and VVIP provision would need to be built. |
| **Verdict** | **Strong fit for its stated purpose as the roundtable and workshop venue.** |

# 5. Venue Relationships

How the Summit's functions relate to each other in space across three days. This is venue logic only — no transport planning.

## 5.1 The two-cluster structure

The RFP's venue requirements resolve naturally into **two functional clusters plus three single-purpose sites**:

| Cluster | Function | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **Evening cluster — SIA** | Broadcast mode: one-to-many, high production, tiered audience | Main stage, backstage, speaker ready room, media area, VIP lounge, VVIP concierge, production compound, break zones |
| **Morning cluster — campus** | Working mode: many-to-many, low production, participatory | Roundtables, workshops, matchmaking, brunch-adjacent space, break and activation zones, demo/exhibition |
| **Social sites** | Gala venue, marina, beach | Single-purpose, single-use, each used once |

**The strategic value of this structure:** the two clusters are used at opposite ends of the day and never compete. Each can be built for its own mode rather than compromised into a hybrid. Guests learn two locations, not eight. Most importantly, **every function that shares a cluster should be co-located within it** — the goal is that once a guest arrives at a cluster, they do not need to leave it until that part of the day is over.

## 5.2 Day-by-day venue logic

**Day 1 — "The State of the Ecosystem"**
`Arrival → Registration → Welcome reception → [SIA: report launch] → [Gala venue]`

Guests move through a linear funnel into the evening cluster, then make **one transition** to the gala. That gala transition is the day's critical constraint: ~300 guests move from a high-production main-stage moment to a separate social venue, immediately after the Summit's signature reveal. The emotional peak must survive the move. **Venue-logic implication: the gala venue's relationship to SIA matters more than its intrinsic quality** — a slightly weaker venue that holds the energy beats a stronger one that dissipates it.

**Day 2 — "Where Growth Happens"**
`[Campus: roundtables] → Brunch → rest → [SIA: evening main stage] → [Marina]`

The most venue-intensive day: the morning cluster, then the evening cluster, then a third social site. **Three distinct locations in one day.** Two implications: brunch should sit as close to the campus as possible to avoid a fourth movement, and the marina transition happens late at night when guest energy is lowest — the shorter and simpler that relationship, the better attendance will hold.

**Day 3 — "From Insight to Action"**
`[Beach: sunrise] → [Campus: workshops] → [SIA: midday close] → Departure`

Structurally the most demanding day despite being the shortest. Guests touch **three venues before mid-afternoon**, starting at dawn and ending with departure. The beach-to-campus movement happens very early; the campus-to-SIA movement lands at midday. **This is where the Day 3 conditions compound** — the closing session falls at the hottest point of the day and at the end of an already fragmented morning, with guests then departing directly afterwards. Venue logic, guest energy and physical conditions all converge on this one session, which is why it is designated a **Site Inspection Priority** (see §4.1). The assessment should account not only for conditions at SIA in isolation, but for the state guests will arrive in after a dawn start and a morning of workshops.

## 5.3 Relationship principles

1. **Minimise transitions, not distances.** Each venue change costs attendance and attention. Cutting one transition is worth more than shortening several.
2. **Cluster by mode, not by proximity.** Functions used together should be co-located, even if that means the two clusters sit far apart.
3. **Never make a guest cross an operational zone.** Production compounds, load-in routes and crew areas must sit outside all guest circulation at every venue.
4. **Preserve one continuous VVIP thread.** Ministers should be able to move arrival → VVIP zone → stage → gala without ever entering general circulation. This is a routing requirement at every venue, not a feature of one.
5. **Every venue needs its own complete support envelope.** Registration presence, wayfinding, welfare, comfort and branding must exist at each site independently — nothing can be shared across venues that are not adjacent.
6. **The main stage is the anchor.** SIA is used on all three days. Its build should be designed once and remain, with the other venues planned around it.
