# Budget Strategy & Itemisation Framework

**RFP reference:** §5.3 *"A detailed, itemized budget broken down by function, with clear assumptions and any optional add-ons priced separately."* · §6 *"Please send a proposal together with an itemized budget."*

> ## ⚠ Status
> **All monetary values in this document are [CLIENT INPUT REQUIRED].** This document establishes the **structure, assumptions and commercial logic** of the budget. Figures are supplied by the commercial team against this framework.
>
> The RFP requires the itemized budget as a **separate accompanying document**. This is the framework it should follow.

---

# 1. Budget Principles

| # | Principle | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Itemised by function, exactly as the RFP asks** | The RFP specifies breakdown by function. Matching its structure makes the budget scoreable rather than interpretable. |
| 2 | **Every assumption stated and numbered** | The RFP explicitly requires "clear assumptions." Unstated assumptions become disputes later. |
| 3 | **Core and optional strictly separated** | The RFP requires add-ons priced separately. Core = required to deliver the brief. Optional = enhancement Entlaq may elect. |
| 4 | **Site-inspection-dependent items flagged, not hidden** | Several venues are unconfirmed. Pricing these silently would be misleading; flagging them demonstrates control. |
| 5 | **Contingency visible, not buried in line items** | Padding individual lines destroys trust. A stated contingency is a professional position. |
| 6 | **Currency, tax and payment terms explicit** | Removes the most common source of commercial ambiguity. |

# 6. Commercial Narrative

Three points the budget presentation should make explicitly:

1. **The budget is structured to the RFP's own scope sections**, so every cost traces directly to a stated requirement. Nothing is unexplained.
2. **Variance is disclosed, not concealed.** Items C, E and O5 carry known variance driven by decisions not yet made. Naming them is more credible than pricing around them.
3. **Optional add-ons are genuinely optional.** Core delivers the brief in full. The ten add-ons are enhancements Entlaq may elect, not scope removed from core to lower a headline number.

## Decision Summary

**Decisions Made**

| Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **Budget structured to the RFP's own scope-of-work sections (A–P)** | Every line traces to a stated requirement; makes the budget scoreable |
| **Core and optional strictly separated, with ten named add-ons** | Directly satisfies the RFP's "optional add-ons priced separately" |
| **24-point assumptions register accompanies the budget** | The RFP explicitly requires clear assumptions |
| **Contingency shown as a visible line, not distributed** | Padded line items destroy trust; a stated contingency is defensible |
| **Known variance disclosed on items C, E and O5** | More credible than pricing around unresolved decisions |
| **Accommodation, flights, F&B, venue hire and talent excluded by default** | None appears in the RFP's scope of work; assumptions stated explicitly |

**Decisions Deferred**

| Deferred | Why |
|---|---|
| All monetary values | Commercial team input required |
| Currency, tax treatment, payment terms | **[CLIENT INPUT REQUIRED]** |
| Contingency percentage | Commercial decision |
| Whether crew accommodation stays in scope | Depends on Entlaq's room-block position |
| Final LED cost | Depends on the Day 3 site assessment |

## Open Client Questions

1. Which elements does Entlaq contract directly — venues, F&B, accommodation, transport?
2. What currency should the budget be presented in, and are figures expected inclusive or exclusive of VAT?
3. What are the expected payment terms and any required proposal validity period?
4. Is there a budget range or ceiling we should design toward?
5. Does crew accommodation sit inside Entlaq's ~150-room block, or is it our cost?
6. Is badge production in our scope, or design only?
7. Who holds responsibility for permits, licences and insurance?
8. Should optional add-ons be presented in the main proposal, or held for post-award discussion?
